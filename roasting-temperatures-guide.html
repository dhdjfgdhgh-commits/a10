Recipe card No. 01

Roasting Temperatures

The right temperature does most of the work. This card gives starting temperatures and times for the things most of us roast, and how to tell when they are done.

You’ll need

  • An oven thermometer
  • A heavy, dark roasting tray
  • A meat thermometer
  • Tongs and a spatula
  • Foil for resting

The method

  1. Check your oven

    Ovens often run hotter or cooler than the dial says. An inexpensive oven thermometer tells you the real temperature, which makes every timing on this card more reliable.

  2. Pick the temperature
    FoodTemperatureApproximate time
    Potatoes, in chunks220°C / 425°F40–50 min
    Root vegetables200°C / 400°F30–40 min
    Broccoli, sprouts220°C / 425°F18–25 min
    Whole chicken (1.5 kg)200°C / 400°Fabout 1 h 20 min
    Chicken thighs, bone-in200°C / 400°F35–45 min
    Fish fillets200°C / 400°F10–15 min
  3. Use a thermometer for meat

    Time is a guide; temperature is the test. Poultry should reach 74°C (165°F) in the thickest part, not touching bone. Follow your local food-safety guidance for other meats and fish.

  4. Rest before carving

    Rest roasts loosely covered with foil: about 10 minutes for thighs and small cuts, 15–20 for a whole chicken and longer for big joints. The juices settle and carving is cleaner.

  5. Fan or conventional

    Fan ovens cook faster. Lower the temperature by about 20°C (25°F) from a conventional recipe and start checking a little early.
