Recipe card No. 02

Roast Vegetables

Roasting turns ordinary vegetables into something sweet, crisp and deeply savoury. A few rules make every tray come out right.

You’ll need

  • Vegetables of your choice
  • 2–3 tbsp oil per tray
  • Salt and pepper
  • Herbs or spices
  • One or two large trays

The method

  1. Cut for even cooking

    Cut dense vegetables (potatoes, carrots, squash) smaller, and quick-cooking ones (peppers, courgettes, mushrooms) larger, so a mixed tray finishes together. Flat cut sides touching the tray brown best.

  2. Dry, then oil

    Pat washed vegetables dry; water on the surface makes them steam. Toss with just enough oil to coat, then salt. Spread them out in a single layer with space between pieces.

  3. Stagger a mixed tray
    • Start first: potatoes, carrots, parsnips, beetroot, squash.
    • Add after 15 minutes: onions, peppers, fennel.
    • Add in the last 15 minutes: broccoli, courgettes, tomatoes, asparagus.
  4. Turn once

    Turn the vegetables once, about halfway through, once the underside has browned. Turning too often keeps them pale.

  5. Finish with flavour

    Add delicate herbs, citrus zest, a squeeze of lemon or grated cheese after roasting; they burn in a hot oven. Garlic cloves are best added whole, in their skins.

Not sure if one tray is enough? The pan checker will tell you.
